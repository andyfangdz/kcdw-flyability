KCDW calibrated wind: research notes

A machine-learned calibration of many weather models' wind forecasts into hourly sustained wind, peak gust and direction at Essex County Airport (KCDW), and at 21 other airports around New York. NBM is used only as a benchmark, never as an input.

What the KCDW model learned →

SHAP attributions, response curves, a beeswarm and the first tree of the production gust model.

What the 22-station model learned →

How one model calibrates each airport differently: per-airport site effects, exposure by wind direction, held-out skill.

Integrity checks

Look-ahead in Open-Meteo archives (ICON, HRES, GEM)Found: "previous day N" used runs up to ~18 h newer than the 00Z runs of every other input, worth 1.8% of gust accuracy. Fixed by removing them; aligned, they added nothing.
Training labels after a test forecast was issued8-day embargo before the test year: +0.06% (negligible).
Leakage across cross-validation month boundariesPurging 7 days around each test month: +0.4% (small).
WeatherNext 2 hindcast overlapping its own training (2022)Masking 2022 made the forecast +0.4% worse, so no leak.
Observation-derived inputsNone among the inputs; every model input is a forecast.
Runs used before they were publishedEach update uses a model run only if it was in our archives by then, from publication delays measured on 2026-09-30 (HRRR about 2 h, GFS about 6 h, WeatherNext 3 about 7 h, the 00Z ECMWF and WeatherNext 2 runs by about 10 h), with margins; a late run falls back to the previous one in training and production alike, and every training row is checked by an assertion.
Same-day forecastsBuilt only from runs published before the update, and only later hours are forecast and scored.
Strict forward yearTrained only before Oct 2025, scored Oct 2025–Sep 2026: gust MAE 3.27 kt, no worse than cross-validation on the same hours (3.29 kt).

What helped

  • Same-day forecasts: using that day's own runs instead of the previous day's cut gust CRPS from 1.889 to 1.790 (-5.2%), and -7.1% for 1–4 pm (forward year).
  • Each model's newest published run at every update (04, 11, 16, 22Z): -1.3% gust MAE overall against the previous production, -4.8% at the midnight update, which used to rely on the previous day's runs.

What did not help

  • NOAA's AI model (GraphCast-GFS, then AIGFS): gust CRPS 1.948 → 1.946 (-0.1%).
  • Boundary-layer depth and winds aloft (native HRRR): gust CRPS 1.870 → 1.865 (-0.2%).
  • Each model's recent error at KCDW: gust CRPS 2.451 → 2.451 (+0.0%).
  • Neural networks: the best (attention across the forecast models) scored +0.8% against XGBoost; averaged 50/50 with XGBoost it improved gust CRPS by 1.0%, too little to justify a second model in production.
  • Training on more airports: from 1 to 22 airports the station-hours grew 21× but the independent days only 1,826 → 1,911; KCDW gust CRPS moved 2.328 → 2.320 (XGBoost).

Still worth knowing

  • More history helps, with diminishing returns: training on the last 1, 2, 3 and 4.3 years gives forward-year gust CRPS 2.502, 2.480, 2.460 and 2.450 (-0.8%, -0.8%, -0.4% per step).
  • Model gust fields: once winds and stability are known, the models' own gust diagnostics add almost nothing.
  • Borrowing from other airports: the 22-station model ties the KCDW-only model at KCDW; its value is calibrating the other airports.
